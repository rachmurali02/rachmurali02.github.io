Cricket

Jul 14, 2019

5 min read

We all saw it in their hands! Didnt We?

Speechless. Sleepless. Heartbroken. When I tried to express what I felt at 2:30 am, it was a turbulent whirlwind of emotions at who I thought was an undeserving winner in that final. It wasn't a run too many. It wasn't a hit too much. It was fortune's game to leave New Zealand a few inches from the cup. We could have rewritten cricketing history the right way.

Watching another game of cricket is going to be difficult. People always told me that "Cricket has too many rules. I don't get them". I never saw what they meant. Today I felt it. The rules that England created seemed to feed off their average cricket, and finally seemed far more daunting to my Black Caps than the players themselves.

When I talk about Ross Taylor, I have a soft corner for my memories of IPL cricket umbilically attached to him. Its the nostalgia of those brilliant knocks that always makes me love his present self. Its his superb slip-catching that makes me celebrate every ball that goes to him in the field. After he had walked off, when that DRS shone with a glaring green, a reflection of the umpires' numerous unpardonable errors that day, I felt the pangs. That elixir of hope had been used on Guptill, and there was none left for Ross.

Rules: 1, NZ: 0

I wasn't going to see him in 2023 this close to a final. It was a tough scratch for Black Caps, but tougher for me.

When Trent Boult bowled a beauty to Jason Roy, first up, I was sure. It was momentary horror to see the umpires remain unfettered by such a beautiful delivery. NZ review. The orange spoils the three-red-unison. The umpire smiles wryly. I am in disbelief. Roy should have been banned from the final in the first place. He should have been back in the dressing room first ball. There is no sense of revenge from the umpires. The orange haunts our Black Caps at their prime. Rules and umpires have joined forces.

Rules + Umpire: 2, NZ: 0

We have to commend the Black Caps' bowling unit for squeezing swing out of a pitch that stopped functioning after a few overs. Henry- Boult spells were peppered with moments of "ooh-aah's". The distant dream looked as close as it can ever be. Ferguson takes a blinder. Butler and Stokes nudge a few. Southee takes a stunner. Butler gone. Has Southee caught the cup???

Plunkett, whose bowling is more ambiguous than an umpire's call, is in the crease. He bowls at 125 kph. Its neither pacy, nor slow. Commentators say cross-seam. I call it a boring dichotomous trick of grip. (Tricks are supposed to be fun, by the way) I felt the English pitch at Lord's liked the ambiguity from Plunkett even when he was batting, to power up rules to combat New Zealand. Simple really.

Rules + Umpires + Plunkett: 3, NZ : 0.

Stokes on strike. He calls 2. Guptill, the one who robbed India of the cup by inches, throws to Latham for a remote chance at a run out. Stokes dives forward with his weapon. The ball, perhaps with the shadows of Dhoni's run out, says "not on my watch", slyly races off the bat and flies for 4. The ball's beaten Ross. Stokes seems apologetic. The heartless umpire calls it "6" RUNS! I exchange looks with my dad. My dad's upset. I am disgusted. Was Karma taunting Guptill?

Fate*(Rules + Umpires) = infinity, NZ : 0

I feel it was more than that. When the Black Caps scrape through exciting semi finals, their excitement works in disharmony with their fatigue. In 2015, when Grant Elliot hit Dale Steyn for a 6 to see New Zealand through to their first final, it was celebration for us. But, since fortune is a one-hit wonder, she lackadaisically backs out: "I am out of office for the next couple of days. You are on your own boys." Fate is all over them, and my Black Caps are thrashed by the Aussies.

Back to the present. It is in those lethargic moments from fortune when New Zealand start to seek solace from a man who is the epitome of hard work, grit, talent, and icy-cool captaincy. Superman: Kane Williamson. If your heart didn't go out to him, are you human? If you didn't rave over his shot selection and trump cards, do you really understand cricket? My heart pounds. He smiles. My legs shake. He chews gum. I can't look at the England team. He pats his opponent's backs.

My rant has ended. The harangue about the embarrassing superover technicalities done up by ICC and bowler-antagonistic rules on boundaries is for another day.

The dream is still alive. Butler, you had to do much more than physically stump Guptill to destroy their ethos as a team. Rules, umpires and fate can only take a tangible cup away, but the intangible sportsmanship and spirit is the real World Cup.

Fate*(Rules + Umpires) = doesn't matter, NZ : Winners

Besides, we all saw it in their hands, and hopefully that is the only thing that really matters. As I see kids from the North and South Islands exuberantly cheer their team on, I am left with a sense of renewed optimism for their road ahead. Go Kiwis!
