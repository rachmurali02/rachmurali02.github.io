1 min read

Ghachar Ghochar

Vivek Shanbhag · translated from Kannada by Srinath Perur

It's fascinating how Ghachar Ghochar explores the idea that rags and riches can harbour so much more than just wealth. What begins as an ordinary household's journey into newfound prosperity slowly unfolds into a story of deep-seated dysfunction, conservatism and familial knots. There's an almost suffocating claustrophobia that comes with their growing wealth, where even the expanses of a larger home feel far more confining than the smaller, humbler house they once lived in. The narrator almost reminisces about poverty with a strange sense of nostalgia, perhaps because having less also meant wanting less.

“When you have no choice, you have no discontent either.”

And perhaps that's the most interesting contradiction in the book: money brings choices, but not necessarily freedom. The family holds itself together through selective acts of blindness and deafness, learning to live with the very things that their newfound wealth has brought upon them.

“It's true what they say — it's not we who control money, it's the money that controls us.”
